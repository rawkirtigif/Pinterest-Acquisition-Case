Executive summary

the short version

Pinterest is growing faster than it has in several years, while its share price has fallen. Revenue rose 18% in Q2 2026 and users reached a record 640 million, yet the stock dropped about 9% after the Q2 report on a softer Q3 outlook, and fell another 9% in September after management flagged international headwinds. This report examines that gap and the company's first major acquisition since 2022.

Growth has reaccelerated

2025 revenue of $4.22B grew 16%, and the first half of 2026 grew about 18%, partly helped by tvScientific.

The upside sits outside North America

Over 80% of users are outside the U.S., but roughly a quarter of revenue comes from outside the U.S. and Canada.

tvScientific is a reasonable but unproven bet

In this report's base case, the deal earns about 7% by 2028, below an assumed 10% cost of capital, so it depends on stronger synergies or a longer payoff period.

Potentially undervalued, under these assumptions

At about 2.2x trailing revenue and 7.4x trailing adjusted EBITDA, the stock sits near the low end of most ranges tested here. That conclusion depends on the assumptions listed later and is not investment advice.

How the business works

from inspiration to checkout

Pinterest describes itself as a visual search and discovery platform positioned at the intersection of search, social, and commerce. It earns almost all of its revenue from ads sold on a cost per click, cost per thousand impressions, or cost per day basis, and since the tvScientific acquisition it also sells ads on connected TV.

The monetization gap

the bull case and the bear case

If Pinterest can lift international revenue per user even partway toward North American levels, revenue can grow without adding users. The timing is uncertain. On its Q2 call, management said pressure from Asia-based cross-border retailers facing European regulation would continue into Q3, and in September the CEO added that the company is rebuilding its international sales organization, which he described as near-term pain.

Competitive landscape

where Pinterest fits

Where Pinterest wins

  • Higher purchase intent than feed-based apps
  • Visual discovery before a shopper knows what to search for
  • A positive, brand-safe environment

Where it is exposed

  • Much smaller scale than Meta, Google, and TikTok

What could go right ▲

  • Access to CTV brand budgets Pinterest could not reach in-app
  • Cross-screen measurement strengthens its case with performance advertisers
  • International rollout adds a second lever after the U.S.

What could go wrong ▼

  • CTV attribution stays contested, keeping budgets with Amazon and Google
  • Integration competes with the international sales rebuild for attention
  • Margin drag lasts beyond 2026 if CTV media costs stay high

Verdict. Strategically logical and financially modest at about a third of one year's free cash flow. Under this report's base case the deal does not clear an assumed 10% cost of capital by 2028, so its value depends on synergies landing toward the high case or paying off over a longer period. CTV revenue and advertiser retention through 2027 are the metrics to watch.

SWOT

risks and catalysts at a glance

Strengths

  • Unique intent and taste data
  • About 99% conversion of 2025 adjusted EBITDA into free cash flow
  • Eleven straight quarters of double-digit user growth

Weaknesses

  • International monetization far behind North America
  • Rising share-based pay keeps GAAP results negative
  • CFO transition at the end of October

Opportunities

  • Visual Search Ads, launched September 2026
  • Connected TV budgets through tvScientific
  • Closing even part of the international revenue-per-user gap

Threats

  • European rules on cross-border sellers
  • AI shopping assistants and Google's visual discovery features
  • A $4B AWS commitment that fixes a large cost base

Strategic recommendations

what I would do next

Go deep in a few markets

Concentrate international sales investment in high-intent markets such as the U.K., Germany, France, and Japan, prove the playbook, then scale. Disclosing ARPU for those markets would give investors the visibility they lack.

Make measurement the product

Package Performance+, Visual Search Ads, and tvScientific into one cross-screen outcome report advertisers can compare directly with Meta and Google.

Diversify European demand

Build local retail and brand partnerships in fashion, beauty, and home, where Pinterest's audience is strongest, to reduce reliance on Asia-based cross-border sellers.

Methodology

how this was built
  • Reported data first. Financials, share counts, debt, leases, commitments, and deal terms come from Pinterest's SEC filings and earnings releases. Third-party sources are used only for prices, peer multiples, estimates, and event coverage.
  • Enterprise value. Equity value at the September 9 close plus convertible notes at principal, less cash and marketable securities. Operating leases are excluded in the base case because adjusted EBITDA is measured after rent, and shown as a sensitivity.
  • Share count. Basic shares from the 10-Q cover; the diluted case adds all unvested RSUs as a conservative upper bound. Out-of-the-money options and notes are excluded.
  • Trailing metrics. Sum of the four most recent reported quarters, Q3 2025 to Q2 2026. Adjusted EBITDA excludes share-based compensation, which was $556M in H1 2026, so EBITDA-based multiples flatter the business.
  • Peers. Reddit and Snap as direct comparables for size and business model; mega-caps excluded from multiples.
  • Deal returns. Third-year after-tax operating return on price plus assumed integration cost, compared with an assumed 10% cost of capital. No terminal value is included, so the framework is a simple screen rather than a full discounted cash flow.
  • Limits. Peer data is not same-day, tvScientific financials are press estimates, and all ranges and scenarios are illustrative.
